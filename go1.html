Is this go for you?

You need: Nothing. Curiosity about how crowds move, and comfort reading a simple chart.

How it works: Pictures first. Every idea is a diagram you can click and a small explorer where you predict an answer, then check it. No code needed and no maths beyond multiplying and dividing.

Quick entry check

1. Crowd density is measured in…

Density is how many people share each square metre. Flow is people per metre of width per second (or per minute).

2. As a corridor gets more crowded, the number of people passing each second…

Flow = density × speed. More people first means more flow, but speed falls, so flow peaks and then drops.

3. Most deadly crowd crushes are caused mainly by…

Investigations point to density and pressure, created by failures of design, information and management, not to 'panic'.

All three right? You could skip ahead to Go 2, or use this go as revision.

The path

  1. 1.1What a crowd is, and why it mattersA crowd is many people sharing one space and often one purpose. Its behaviour comes from simple local rules, and its risks from too many people in too little space.30 min
    Big picture
  2. 1.2Space, density and level of serviceDensity is people per square metre; its inverse is the space each person has. Fruin's levels of service turn space per person into grades from A (free) to F (breakdown).35 min
    Measure
  3. 1.3Speed, flow and the fundamental diagramFlow = density × speed. As density rises, speed falls, so flow peaks (capacity) and then drops. This curve, the fundamental diagram, is the backbone of crowd science.40 min
    Core idea
  4. 1.4Bottlenecks and capacityThe narrowest point on a route sets the flow for the whole route. Capacity grows with width, about 1.3 to 1.9 people per metre per second in experiments, and queues grow when demand exceeds it.35 min
    Core idea
  5. 1.5Self-organisation: lanes, oscillations and wavesWithout a leader, crowds form lanes in counterflow, take turns at narrow doors, and make stop-and-go waves at high density. Simple local rules create these patterns.30 min
    Patterns
  6. 1.6People, not particles: behaviour and choicePeople choose routes, walk in groups, follow familiar paths and respond to information. Crowd behaviour is social, and 'panic' is rare. Good management works with that behaviour.35 min
    Behaviour
  7. 1.7When crowds become dangerousRisk rises steeply with density. Above about 5 to 6 people per m² people lose control of their movement; forces add up through bodies, and crowd turbulence and collapse can follow.40 min
    Risk
  8. 1.8Planning and managing safe crowdsSafe events are designed, informed and managed in every phase. DIM-ICE and RAMP structure the risk analysis; flow rates and egress times size the exits.40 min
    Practice

Spoken overview
Read the transcript

A cellular automaton turns the floor into a chessboard of small cells, typically forty centimetres across, the space one person occupies in a dense crowd. Each cell is empty or holds one person. Every time step, about a third of a second, each person may hop to a free neighbouring cell. Which cell? That depends on floor fields. The static floor field is like a slope towards the exit: cells nearer the exit are more attractive. The dynamic floor field is like footprints in snow: people leave a trail that fades over time, and others are drawn to follow it, which reproduces lanes and herding. Two numbers, kS and kD, set how strongly people respond to each field. With strong kS everyone heads straight for the exit; with weak kS people wander. The model is fast and easy to code, which made it popular, as in the floor-field model of Burstedde and colleagues in two thousand and one. But it needs care: in its simplest form it lets people leave through a door much faster than real crowds do. Calibration fixes that.

A floor of cells

A cellular automaton (CA) is a grid of cells with simple local rules. For crowds, cells are usually 0.4 m × 0.4 m, about the space of one person in a dense crowd, so full occupancy is 6.25 per m². Each cell is empty or holds one person, which automatically prevents overlaps. Time moves in steps of about 0.3 s, giving a free speed of about 1.3 m/s.

Static and dynamic floor fields

Burstedde et al. (2001) guided people with two fields. The static field S measures distance to the exit and does not change. The dynamic field D is a virtual trail: each move adds to it, and it decays and diffuses over time. A person chooses among free neighbour cells with probability

p ∝ exp(−kS × S) × exp(kD × D)

Large kS: people know the way and head straight for it. Large kD: people follow others (herding), useful for smoke or unfamiliar buildings.

Explore it

Set kS = 0: people wander randomly and take far longer. Then raise kD and watch trails form behind early leavers.

Update rules and time

In random sequential update, people move one at a time in a fresh random order each step; this is simple and avoids conflicts. In parallel update everyone moves at once, and two people may target the same cell: a conflict rule decides who wins, and a friction parameter can make both stay (Kirchner, Nishinari and Schadschneider 2003). Parallel update with friction reproduces clogging at exits.

Calibrate before you trust it

Run the plain model and count the exit flow. With an exit two cells wide (0.8 m) the lab model empties 150 people in about 37 s: over 5 people per metre per second, far above the 1.3 to 1.9 measured in experiments. One crude fix is an exit probability: a person on an exit cell leaves with probability q per step. With q = 0.2 the flow falls to about 1.35 per m per s. Real studies calibrate several outputs at once (module 2.8).

Nice pictures, wrong numbers

A CA can look convincing while getting capacity badly wrong. Always compare flows with data before using results.

Calibrating the exit flowPythonDownload
"""Lab 2.5: the same evacuation three ways: a hydraulic hand calculation, a macroscopic estimate and the cellular automaton of lab 2.3."""
from m23_ca import evacuate

N, width, dist, v = 150, 0.8, 10.0, 1.3       # people, exit width (m), mean distance to exit (m), free speed (m/s)
js = 1.3                                      # specific flow through the exit (per m per s)
t_hyd = dist / v + N / (js * width)           # walk to the exit, then queue through it
print(f"hydraulic estimate: {t_hyd:.0f} s")
for q in [1.0, 0.3, 0.2]:
    steps = evacuate(N=N, kS=2.0, kD=0.5, exit_w=2, q_exit=q)   # 2 cells of 0.4 m = 0.8 m
    print(f"cellular automaton, exit probability {q}: {steps} steps = {steps * 0.3:.0f} s, "
          f"exit flow {N / (steps * 0.3) / width:.2f} per m per s")
print("Experiments: about 1.3 to 1.9 per m per s. The plain CA lets one person leave per exit cell every step;")
print("an exit probability (a crude stand-in for friction, Kirchner et al. 2003) brings it into the measured range.")

Remember

  • A floor-field CA moves people between 0.4 m cells in steps of about 0.3 s.
  • The static field points to the exit; the dynamic field is a fading trail; kS and kD weight them.
  • Update rules and friction decide conflicts and clogging.
  • The plain CA's exit flow is far too high: calibrate before use.

Words and links

New words

Cellular automaton
A grid model where each cell's state changes by local rules each step.
Static floor field
A fixed field, such as distance to the exit, that attracts people.
Dynamic floor field
A virtual trail left by moving people that decays and diffuses.
Random sequential update
Moving agents one at a time in a random order each step.
Friction parameter
In parallel-update CAs, the chance that conflicting people both stay put.

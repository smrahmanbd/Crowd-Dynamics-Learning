Spoken overview
Read the transcript

In nineteen ninety-five Dirk Helbing and Péter Molnár described pedestrians as if they were particles moved by forces. Not physical forces, at first, but social ones: each person feels a pull towards their goal at their desired speed, and a push away from other people and walls that grows quickly as they get closer. The pull works like a spring: if you are slower than you want to be, you accelerate, over a relaxation time of about half a second. In two thousand, Helbing, Farkas and Vicsek added physical forces for when bodies actually touch: a body force that resists squeezing, and sliding friction. With these, the model shows something surprising. When people try to leave a room very fast, they press into the door, arches of bodies form and block it, and the room empties more slowly. Faster is slower. The social force model reproduces lanes, oscillations at doors and clogging. Its weaknesses are many parameters, sensitivity to time steps, and people who can overlap or wobble if parameters are poorly chosen.

The parts

The main parameters and typical values (Helbing, Farkas and Vicsek 2000).

PartWhat it doesThink of it as
Desired speed v₀Speed people want: about 0.6 m/s (relaxed) to 1.5 (normal) or more (hurried)The cruise control setting
Relaxation time τHow quickly people adapt their velocity: about 0.5 sHow fast you react on a bike
A and BStrength (2,000 N) and range (0.08 m) of social repulsionPersonal-space bubble
k and κBody force and friction coefficients when bodies touchStiffness and grip of a rubber ball
Radius rBody radius, about 0.25 to 0.35 mShoulder half-width

The equation

For person i with mass m, velocity v and desired velocity v0e:

m dvi/dt = m (v0ei − vi) ÷ τ + Σj fij + ΣW fiW

The first term pulls the person to their desired velocity over a relaxation time τ. The others are interaction forces with other people j and walls W (Helbing and Molnár 1995).

Repulsion and contact

Helbing, Farkas and Vicsek (2000) used

fij = [A exp((rij − dij) ÷ B) + k g(rij − dij)] nij + κ g(rij − dij) Δvtji tij

where rij is the sum of radii, dij the distance, n and t the normal and tangent, and g(x) = x when bodies overlap, else 0. The first part is social repulsion; the k and κ terms act only on contact.

Faster is slower

In the lab with 100 people and a 0.8 m door, raising the desired speed from 1 to 2 m/s halves the evacuation time (85 s to 43 s), 4 m/s helps a little (40 s), but 6 m/s is slower (52 s): people press into the door, form arches and block each other. Helbing, Farkas and Vicsek (2000) called this faster-is-slower. Experiments with pushing crowds and animals show related effects, though the details remain debated.

Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths: continuous space, intuitive forces, reproduces lanes, oscillations and clogging; widely implemented. Weaknesses: many parameters, stiff equations needing small time steps (about 0.005 s with contact forces), people who can overlap or oscillate, and speeds that do not drop to zero at high density without extra care. Many variants exist; always state which you use and how you calibrated it.

Time step

With contact forces, a time step of 0.05 s can let people pass through each other. The lab uses 0.005 s.

"""Lab 2.4: social force model (Helbing and Molnar 1995; Helbing, Farkas and Vicsek 2000), a room with one door.
Explore 'faster is slower': raise the desired speed v0 and watch the evacuation time."""
import numpy as np

def run(v0=1.2, N=60, door=1.0, A=2000.0, B=0.08, k=1.2e5, kap=2.4e5, m=80.0, tau=0.5, dt=0.005, T=120, seed=2):
    rng = np.random.default_rng(seed)
    W, H = 12.0, 8.0
    pos = []
    while len(pos) < N:
        p = rng.uniform([0.5, 0.5], [8, H - 0.5])
        if all(np.hypot(*(p - q)) > 0.55 for q in pos): pos.append(p)
    x = np.array(pos); v = np.zeros_like(x); r = rng.uniform(0.25, 0.30, N); out = np.zeros(N, bool); t_out = []
    y0, y1 = H / 2 - door / 2, H / 2 + door / 2
    for step in range(int(T / dt)):
        idx = np.where(~out)[0]
        if len(idx) == 0: break
        X, V, R = x[idx], v[idx], r[idx]
        gy = np.clip(X[:, 1], y0 + 0.25, y1 - 0.25)
        goal = np.where((X[:, 0] < W - 0.3)[:, None], np.c_[np.full(len(idx), W), gy], np.c_[np.full(len(idx), W + 3), np.full(len(idx), H / 2)])
        e = goal - X; e /= np.linalg.norm(e, axis=1, keepdims=True)
        F = m * (v0 * e - V) / tau
        d = X[:, None, :] - X[None, :, :]; dist = np.linalg.norm(d, axis=2) + np.eye(len(idx))
        n_ = d / dist[..., None]; rr = R[:, None] + R[None, :]; g = np.clip(rr - dist, 0, None)
        rep = (A * np.exp((rr - dist) / B) + k * g) * (1 - np.eye(len(idx)))
        tvec = np.stack([-n_[..., 1], n_[..., 0]], -1); dvt = ((V[None, :, :] - V[:, None, :]) * tvec).sum(-1)
        F += (rep[..., None] * n_).sum(1) + ((kap * g * dvt)[..., None] * tvec).sum(1)
        for wy in (0.0, H):                                   # top and bottom walls
            dd = np.abs(X[:, 1] - wy); nn = np.sign(X[:, 1] - wy)[:, None] * np.array([0, 1])
            F += ((A * np.exp((R - dd) / B) + k * np.clip(R - dd, 0, None))[:, None]) * nn
        inwall = (np.abs(X[:, 0] - W) < 1) & ((X[:, 1] < y0) | (X[:, 1] > y1))   # wall with the door gap
        dd = np.abs(X[:, 0] - W); F[:, 0] += np.where(inwall, -(A * np.exp((R - dd) / B) + k * np.clip(R - dd, 0, None)), 0)
        V = V + F / m * dt; s = np.linalg.norm(V, axis=1, keepdims=True); V = np.where(s > 1.3 * v0 + 0.2, V / s * (1.3 * v0 + 0.2), V)
        x[idx] = X + V * dt; v[idx] = V
        left = idx[x[idx, 0] > W + 0.3]; out[left] = True; t_out += [step * dt] * len(left)
    return (max(t_out) if out.all() else None), len(t_out)

if __name__ == "__main__":
    # 100 people, 0.8 m door: with these settings 6 m/s is slower than 4 m/s ("faster is slower"). Takes a few minutes.
    for v0 in [1.0, 2.0, 4.0, 6.0]:
        T, n = run(v0=v0, N=100, door=0.8, T=200)
        print(f"v0 = {v0} m/s: {n} out, last after {T if T else '>200'} s")

Remember

  • The social force model: driving force + repulsion from people and walls + contact forces.
  • Typical values: τ ≈ 0.5 s, A ≈ 2,000 N, B ≈ 0.08 m (Helbing et al. 2000).
  • It reproduces lanes, clogging and faster-is-slower.
  • It has many parameters and stiff equations: use small time steps and calibrate.

Words and links

New words

Social force model
A microscopic model where people move under a driving force and interaction forces (Helbing and Molnár 1995).
Desired speed
The speed a person wants to walk at (v₀).
Relaxation time
The time over which a person adapts their velocity (τ).
Faster-is-slower
The effect that higher desired speeds can slow an evacuation through clogging.
Arching
A blocking arch of people that forms in front of a narrow exit.

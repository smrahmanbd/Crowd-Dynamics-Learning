Speed, flow and the fundamental diagram

Flow = density × speed. As density rises, speed falls, so flow peaks (capacity) and then drops. This curve, the fundamental diagram, is the backbone of crowd science.

Spoken overview
Read the transcript

Imagine a corridor. With a few people, everyone walks at their own comfortable speed, about one point three metres per second. Add more people and two things happen. More people pass each second, because there are more of them, but each person slows down, because others are in the way. Flow, the number of people crossing a line each second per metre of width, is density times speed. At first density wins and flow rises. Then speed falls so fast that flow stops rising, reaches a maximum, and falls again. That maximum is the capacity of the corridor, about one point two people per metre per second in the classic curve by Ulrich Weidmann, reached at a density of nearly two people per square metre. The curve of flow against density is called the fundamental diagram. It is the single most important picture in crowd dynamics, and every model you meet later must reproduce it.

تخيّل ممراً. عندما يكون فيه عدد قليل من الناس، يمشي كل واحد بسرعته المريحة، نحو متر وثلاثة أعشار المتر في الثانية. أضف مزيداً من الناس فيحدث أمران. يمرّ عدد أكبر من الناس كل ثانية لأنهم أكثر، لكن كل شخص يبطئ لأن الآخرين يعترضون طريقه. والتدفق، أي عدد الأشخاص الذين يعبرون خطاً كل ثانية لكل متر من العرض، يساوي الكثافة مضروبة في السرعة. في البداية تتغلب الكثافة فيرتفع التدفق. ثم تنخفض السرعة بسرعة كبيرة فيتوقف التدفق عن الارتفاع، ويبلغ حداً أقصى، ثم ينخفض من جديد. وهذا الحد الأقصى هو سعة الممر، نحو شخص وخُمس لكل متر في الثانية في المنحنى الكلاسيكي لأولريش فايدمان، ويُبلغ عند كثافة تقارب شخصين في المتر المربع. ويُسمّى منحنى التدفق مقابل الكثافة المخطط الأساسي. وهو أهم صورة في ديناميكا الحشود، وكل نموذج ستتعرف عليه لاحقاً يجب أن يعيد إنتاجه.

Flow = density × speed

Think of a corridor 1 m wide. If density is 1 person per m² and everyone walks at 1 m/s, one person crosses a line each second. The rule is:

J = ρ × v   (people per metre of width per second)

For a corridor of width b, the total flow is J × b. Example: ρ = 2 per m², v = 0.6 m/s, b = 3 m gives J = 1.2 per m per s and a total of 3.6 people per second, about 216 per minute.

Speed falls as density rises

Alone, adults walk at about 1.34 m/s on average (Weidmann 1993). With others around, they slow down. Weidmann summarised 25 studies with a formula:

v(ρ) = 1.34 × [1 − exp(−1.913 × (1/ρ − 1/5.4))] m/s

At 1 per m² it gives about 1.06 m/s, at 2 per m² about 0.61 m/s, and at 5.4 per m² zero: everyone stands still.

The fundamental diagram

Multiply speed by density and plot flow against density: the curve rises, peaks and falls. The peak is capacity: about 1.22 people per metre per second at about 1.75 per m² in Weidmann's curve. Left of the peak the flow is free; right of the peak it is congested, and adding people reduces flow.

This is why a corridor operated near capacity is fragile: a small disturbance can push it onto the congested branch, where it moves fewer people and density keeps rising.

Capacity in practice

A 4 m wide passage at 1.2 per m per s carries 4.8 people per second, about 290 per minute or 17,000 per hour. Guidance documents use similar or lower planning values for safety.

One curve does not fit all crowds

Measured diagrams differ. Single-file experiments in Jülich (Seyfried et al. 2005), crowds in India and Germany (Chattaraj, Seyfried and Chakroborty 2009) and pilgrims at the Jamarat Bridge (Helbing et al. 2007) all give different curves. Purpose matters too: commuters walk faster than shoppers, and two-way flows behave differently from one-way flows.

Borrowing a curve

Using one fundamental diagram everywhere can overestimate capacity for older or slower crowds, such as many pilgrims.

Remember

  • Specific flow J = density × speed; total flow = J × width.
  • Speed falls with density, from about 1.34 m/s to zero near 5.4 per m² (Weidmann).
  • Flow peaks at capacity (about 1.2 per m per s) then falls: the fundamental diagram.
  • Fundamental diagrams vary with culture, purpose and direction of flow.

Words and links

New words

Speed
How fast people walk, in metres per second.
Specific flow
People passing per metre of width per second (J = ρ × v).
Fundamental diagram
The relation between density and flow (or speed) of a crowd.
Capacity
The maximum flow a facility can carry.
Free speed
The speed people walk when unhindered, about 1.34 m/s on average (Weidmann).
Jam density
The density at which movement stops; 5.4 per m² in Weidmann's curve.

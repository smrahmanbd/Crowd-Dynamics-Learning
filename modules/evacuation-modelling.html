Evacuation modelling: RSET and ASET

Spoken overview
Read the transcript

Fire engineers ask a simple question: will everyone get out before conditions become dangerous? The time available, called ASET, comes from fire and smoke modelling. The time required, called RSET, is the sum of four parts: the time to detect the fire, the time to warn people, the pre-movement time while people notice, decide and gather their things or their families, and the movement time to walk to and through the exits. A design is acceptable when RSET is clearly shorter than ASET. Movement time can be estimated by hand with the hydraulic method: the walking time, or the time for the crowd to flow through the exits, whichever is longer. Simulators add detail. But the largest and most uncertain part is usually pre-movement. People finish their conversation, look for friends, or wait for confirmation. Experiments and real evacuations show long, skewed distributions of pre-movement times. That is why careful studies treat RSET not as one number but as a distribution, and look at its high percentiles.

Two clocks

Performance-based fire engineering compares two times. ASET, the available safe egress time, runs until smoke, heat or toxic gases make conditions untenable; it comes from fire modelling. RSET, the required safe egress time, is how long people need:

RSET = tdetection + twarning + tpre-movement + tmovement

The design is acceptable if RSET < ASET with an adequate safety margin (see the SFPE Handbook and PD 7974-6).

Movement time by hand

The hydraulic method takes the longer of walking time and flow time:

tmovement ≈ max( distance ÷ speed, people ÷ (specific flow × exit width) )

Example: 1,500 people, 6 m of exits at 1.3 per m per s, 60 m at 1.2 m/s: walking 50 s, flow 1,500 ÷ 7.8 ≈ 192 s, so movement ≈ 192 s. With detection 60 s, warning 30 s and pre-movement 120 s, RSET ≈ 402 s, about 6.7 minutes.

Pre-movement time

People rarely move the moment an alarm sounds. They look for information, finish tasks, find their group. Measured pre-movement times are often long-tailed: most people start within a minute or two, some much later. Pre-movement depends on alertness (asleep or awake), familiarity, staff behaviour and the alarm: voice messages that say what to do shorten it compared with bells.

Zero pre-movement

Simulations that start everyone moving at time zero can underestimate RSET badly. Always add a realistic pre-movement distribution.

RSET as a distribution

Uncertain inputs make RSET uncertain. Sample them many times (Monte Carlo): in the lab, a lognormal pre-movement around 120 s and uncertain flow and speed give RSET percentiles of about 323 s (5th), 409 s (50th) and 569 s (95th). Compare a high percentile, not the average, with ASET, and report how sensitive the result is to each input.

Words and links

New words

RSET
Required safe egress time: detection + warning + pre-movement + movement.
ASET
Available safe egress time, until conditions become untenable.
Pre-movement time
Time from warning to the start of purposeful movement.
Hydraulic method
Movement time from flow rates and walking speeds.
Monte Carlo
Repeated random sampling of uncertain inputs to get a distribution of results.

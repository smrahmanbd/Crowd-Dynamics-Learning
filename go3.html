Is this go for you?

You need: Go 2, or its exit check. Python; for full studies, an open-source simulator such as JuPedSim or Vadere.

How it works: Each module reads like a research brief: the question, the evidence, the method, the implementation in code, and the pitfalls and ethics.

Quick entry check

1. 'Crowd turbulence' means…

Helbing and colleagues observed it at the Jamarat Bridge in 2006: pressure waves that push people over.

2. Scheduling pilgrim groups to arrive at different times aims to…

Spreading arrivals lowers the peak demand on each route, so density stays lower.

3. Crowd pressure, as defined by Helbing and colleagues, is…

Pressure = ρ · Var(V): high only when the crowd is both dense and moving irregularly.

All three right? You are ready for the research modules. Not sure? Revisit Go 2.

What you will produce

  • A research proposal for a mass-gathering crowd study, with a scenario matrix and a data plan
  • A density and crowd-pressure monitoring pipeline on trajectory or video-derived data
  • A simulation study of an intervention (metering, one-way routing or scheduling), with sensitivity analysis
  • A short paper and talk that state clearly what the evidence can and cannot show

The path

  1. 3.1Framing a mass-gathering crowd studyA good study starts with a sharp question, a clear outcome measure, a scenario design that covers what matters, a data plan and an ethics plan. This module sets up the Go 3 research project.45 min
    Research brief
  2. 3.2Lessons from crowd disastersInvestigations of Hillsborough, Love Parade, Mina and Itaewon show systemic failures of design, information and management. Analyse them with DIM-ICE and the published evidence, not with hindsight stories of panic.50 min
    Evidence
  3. 3.3The Hajj as a crowd systemClose to two million pilgrims follow one ritual sequence across Mina, Muzdalifah and Arafat. Infrastructure (the multi-level Jamarat), one-way routing and time-slot scheduling of groups are the main tools.50 min
    Case study
  4. 3.4Data and sensing at mass gatheringsCounting very large crowds combines area-and-density methods, computer vision on CCTV and drone images, and phone data. Every method has errors; quantify them, and protect privacy.45 min
    Data
  5. 3.5Real-time monitoring and early warningControl rooms turn camera and sensor data into density, flow and crowd-pressure maps, and act on thresholds before conditions become critical. Warning levels must be tested and tied to actions.45 min
    Operations
  6. 3.6Simulating a mass gatheringBuild the geometry, set demand and schedules, choose and calibrate a model, run many seeds and scenarios, and analyse sensitivity. Open simulators such as JuPedSim make this reproducible.50 min
    Methods
  7. 3.7Interventions and their evaluationMetering, one-way systems, holding areas, scheduling and information can keep density down, but they move risk as well as reduce it. Evaluate them with simulation and data, including side effects.45 min
    Interventions
  8. 3.8The research frontier and writing it upOpen questions remain: pushing and high-density physics, the psychology of crowds, AI for monitoring, digital twins, and heat and health at mass gatherings. Report findings honestly, with uncertainty and limits.40 min
    Research

An open course on crowd dynamics and crowd safety, taught in three goes, with mass gatherings as the research case. Facts follow the cited literature and guidance (Fruin, Weidmann, Helbing and colleagues, Seyfried and colleagues, Still, and the Green Guide, among others). Explorers and toy models are teaching versions with illustrative numbers: never use them on their own to size or approve a real venue.
